Pasta Carbonara

Description

Spaghetti alla Carbonara is a quintessential Roman pasta dish celebrated for its rich, velvety sauce created without any cream. The magic lies in the emulsion of raw egg yolks, finely grated Pecorino Romano, rendered pork fat, and starchy pasta cooking water, which yields a glossy coating over perfectly al dente pasta.

Crisp bits of cured guanciale provide savory depth and texture, while generous amounts of freshly cracked black pepper cut through the richness. The entire dish hinges on residual heat to gently cook the eggs into a silky sauce rather than scrambling them.

Ingredients

  • 400 g spaghetti
  • 150 g guanciale (or thick-cut unsmoked pancetta), cut into matchsticks
  • 4 large egg yolks plus 1 whole egg
  • 80 g Pecorino Romano cheese, freshly grated (plus extra for serving)
  • Coarsely ground black pepper
  • Coarse salt for the pasta water

Steps

  1. Bring a large pot of water to a gentle boil and season lightly with salt. Cook the spaghetti until 1–2 minutes shy of al dente.
  2. While the pasta cooks, add the guanciale to a cold skillet and cook over medium-low heat until the fat renders and the meat is crisp and golden. Remove the skillet from heat and let it cool slightly.
  3. In a bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, whole egg, grated Pecorino Romano, and a generous amount of cracked black pepper until a thick paste forms.
  4. Reserve 1 cup of starchy pasta water, then drain the spaghetti and transfer it directly into the skillet with the rendered guanciale and fat. Toss to coat over low heat for 30 seconds.
  5. Take the skillet completely off the heat. Pour the egg and cheese mixture over the pasta, tossing rapidly and adding small splashes of reserved pasta water as needed until a creamy, glossy emulsion coats every strand. Serve immediately with extra Pecorino and black pepper.
